Koḍi Habba at Koṭeśvara

The great chariot festival of Śrī Koṭiliṅgeśvara at Koṭeśvara, near Kundapura, on the full moon of Vṛścika.

Koḍi Habba is the great chariot festival, the Mahārathotsava, of Śrī Koṭiliṅgeśvara at Koṭeśvara, four kilometres south of Kundapura in Udupi district. It falls on the full moon of the lunar month of Kārttika or Mārgaśira that falls in the solar month of Vṛścika. The sthala-purāṇa says that on this day Śiva appeared to Brahmā, the gods and the sages as a crore of liṅgas, the Koṭiliṅga that gives the temple and the town their names.

How old the festival is

The Koḍi Habba is first named in an inscription dated 20 October 1373, when the Barkur kingdom was governed for the Vijayanagara king Bukka: it records a gift of land to provide meals for holy men “at the Kodi Habba”. The festival has been held for more than six and a half centuries.

How old the temple is

The temple is older still. Its earliest dated inscription, in the inner enclosure, was written on 22 November 1261, under the Ālupa kings who ruled this coast for more than a thousand years; sixteen inscriptions of 950–1350 CE and thirty-two of the Vijayanagara period (1336–1600) have been found here. One image in the temple, a white marble goddess, has been dated by one scholar to the 10th–11th century, and fragments of older, unreadable Ālupa inscriptions suggest that worship here goes back further still. Tradition holds that the temple was built by Emperor Vasu, who had no children; the purāṇa tells how Vasu first held the chariot festival here, on the full moon of Vṛścika, with a chariot of twelve wheels.

The name

The festival keeps the town's oldest name. The inscriptions call Koṭeśvara Kuḍikuru; koḍi means a tip of land where a river meets the sea, and the festival of Koḍiyūru is the Koḍi Habba. The purāṇas call the town Dhvajapura, the town of the banner, for the flag that Vasu raised before the temple.

Seven days

The flag is hoisted on the ninth day of the bright fortnight. Each evening the deity goes out on a different vehicle: peacock, serpent, bull, elephant, horse and, on the sixth night, the lion. On the seventh day, the full moon, comes the great chariot. A festival of this length is called a Bhuvanotsava, held for the welfare of all living beings.
